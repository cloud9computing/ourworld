# Our World: question sets by how long a couple has been together

This replaces the question bank and tier rules in the PRD (section 7) and in PRD changes v3 (R1). Each tier gets a fixed set of 12 questions. Every question has the place it asks for and one "ask your partner" prompt that appears on its reveal card (R2 in v3). Each prompt needs one or two more variants before launch so replays don't repeat.

## Tiers

The "How long have you been together?" chips become:

| Chip | Who it's for |
|---|---|
| Just met | The first few dates |
| Under a year | Getting serious, still learning the stories |
| 1–3 years | Some trips together, a shared life forming |
| 3–7 years | A lot shared; old stories half-told |
| 7–15 years | Think they know everything |
| 15+ years | A whole life together, two childhoods ago |

The chip is now **required** (one tap), because "Just met" changes the whole structure.

## What each tier is most curious about

These come from role-playing a couple at each stage.

- **Just met.** They know each other's job and maybe a hometown, and nothing else. Shared places are almost zero. What's fun is first impressions: guessing about the other, and finding out who the other person is. It has to stay light. Nothing about the future that would feel like pressure on a third date, and nothing about past relationships.
- **Under a year.** They've heard the headline stories, but not where they happened. They want to understand each other's family and roots, and the first trips together are precious and freshly remembered.
- **1–3 years.** There's a shared life now, with some trips and maybe a home together. The fun is how differently they remember the same things, and starting to dream together.
- **3–7 years.** Daily life has taken over. Old stories are half-told, and some dreams were parked. They're curious about the parts of each other's past that never came up, and what each other secretly still wants.
- **7–15 years.** They think they know everything, and are delighted when they don't. The gold is childhood places the other has never seen, the person they were before, and dreams put off because life got busy. Romance worth reviving: "where you fell for me all over again".
- **15+ years.** Their childhoods feel very far away, and their early days together have become legends. They're curious about the young version of each other, the places they'd revisit, and where to grow old.

Across all tiers:
- **Questions about the partner are the best moments.** "Where you saw me happiest" works at every stage except "Just met".
- **Guessing is only for "Just met".** Nobody expects a new date to know the answers, so guessing is playful. For long-term couples a guess can feel like a test, which breaks the no-scoring rule.
- **Every answer must be a place.** Keep every question answerable with one city in a few seconds.
- **No plain facts after "Just met".** From "Under a year" on, every question is a superlative, an opinion, a memory or a view of the partner. Test: if a partner could predict the answer almost every time (where you grew up, where your family is from, where we met), rewrite it. Plain facts are only used in "Just met", where they are genuinely new.
- **Off-limits in every tier:** past partners, loss and anything that could feel like a test of what the other person knows.

**Special rules for "Just met"**
- **Guess questions (6 and 7) are linked** to the partner's own answers to questions 1 and 2. The reveal shows the guess next to the real answer: "Kabir guessed Mumbai. Aanya grew up in Jaipur." A correct guess gets the match celebration, and a wrong one gets warm copy ("Now you know") with the prompt.
- **No "Been there together?" step.** Every place from the other's world goes straight into the explore list, unless both named it.
- The labelled star on the poster is a place both partners named, if any. Otherwise no place is labelled.
- The poster hero stays "places still to explore in each other's worlds", which suits new couples best of all.

## Checks every set must pass

Each set above was checked against these. Re-run them whenever a question changes.

1. **Fast start:** each act opens with a concrete question answerable in under 10 seconds.
2. **Few abstract questions:** at most two per set ("feels like home", "most like yourself", "knew this was something", "changed us").
3. **Beyond the home city:** at least 6 of the 12 answers would usually be somewhere other than the city the couple lives in. Act 1 and Act 3 carry most of this; Act 2 includes trip-based questions.
4. **No ex-shaped doors:** no question or prompt where an honest answer naturally involves a past partner.
5. **Low-stakes futures:** future questions are dreams (a month a year, a summer, a year somewhere), never decisions (where we will live, where we will grow old).
6. **No blame:** nothing implies one partner held the other back.
7. **No back-to-back heavy questions.** "The trip that changed us" is the only heavy one, and it follows a warm one.
8. **Little overlap:** no two questions in a set are likely to get the same answer most of the time. "A place from your past you want to show me" shows the "Somewhere you haven't named yet" nudge on its chips.

Known trade-off: "Where you knew this was something" (under a year) may put pressure on a partner who isn't sure yet. Keep it, make the skip feel casual, and soften it to "Where you first thought 'I like this person'" if testing shows awkwardness.

## Rules for Claude Code

- **Fixed sets:** each tier uses its 12 questions in the order shown. Both phones get the same set from the tier in the invite code.
- **Optional lists:** the two list questions ("Any other places we've been?" and "Every city we've lived in together") still follow the 12. For "Just met", the first becomes "Any other places you love?", answered separately by each partner, and the second is skipped.
- **Explore list:**
  - Act 1 answers feed the explore list through the "Been there together?" step, except in "Just met" (above).
  - All Act 3 answers feed it directly.
  - "A place from our early days we should go back to" (15+, question 8) is a shared past place. It also goes into the explore list, labelled "somewhere to go back to".
- **Where each prompt shows:** on the reveal card, addressed to the partner who did not give the answer ("Ask Ravi: ..."). For "about partner" questions, the prompt is addressed to the person the answer is about.
- **Copy review before launch:** no question or prompt mentions past partners, loss or a score.
